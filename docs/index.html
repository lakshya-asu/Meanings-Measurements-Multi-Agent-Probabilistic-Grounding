Abstract

Robots collaborating with humans need to convert natural language goals into actionable, physically grounded decisions. A command like "go two meters to the right of the fridge" requires grounding a semantic reference, a spatial relation, and a metric constraint all at once, inside a 3D scene. Existing vision-language grounding systems often produce discrete actions or single target hypotheses rather than metric-semantic goal densities. In our frozen evaluation, adapted GraphEQA produces lower horizontal SR@2m than MAPG under matched backends, although the adapted interfaces differ.


To address this, we propose MAPG (Multi-Agent Probabilistic Grounding), an agentic framework that decomposes language queries into structured subcomponents and queries a VLM to ground each one. MAPG probabilistically composes these grounded outputs into a goal density, then selects the navmesh-masked argmax as the planner goal point. We evaluate MAPG on MAPG-Bench, a frozen protocol with 98 object-to-world queries across 41 HM3D scenes, and report a backend sensitivity check on 94 runnable HM-EQA questions. The current results are exploratory, single-seed measurements rather than a completed multi-seed confirmatory analysis. A real-world robot demonstration illustrates the approach outside simulation when a structured scene representation is available.

Contributions

  • A multi-agent probabilistic 3D spatial reasoning framework that couples online 3D scene graphs with analytically defined continuous spatial kernels, then selects the navmesh-masked argmax as a planner goal for metric-semantic instructions.
  • MAPG-Bench, an HM3D goal-grounding benchmark with a frozen registered split of 98 object-to-world queries across 41 indoor scenes. We plan to release the dataset and evaluation protocol with versioned corrections for known annotation issues.
  • Exploratory ground-truth results under one frozen seed and split. Across five unoptimized VLM backends, horizontal SR@1m ranges from 17.35% to 21.43% and horizontal SR@2m from 40.82% to 43.88%. The 3D SR@1m range is 5.10% to 6.12%. These metrics evaluate waypoint correctness, not the model's self-reported confidence.

Method

MAPG is built around a modular pipeline of specialized agents that share a common memory ledger and operate over a 3D scene graph of the environment. Given a natural language instruction, the system decomposes it, resolves referents, generates spatial distributions, and composes them into a single planner-ready goal density.

1. Orchestrator. The Orchestrator parses a free-form instruction into structured Spatial Description Clauses (SDCs): an anchor object, a spatial predicate (e.g., "right of"), and a metric constraint (e.g., "2 meters"). For the query "Where is 2 meters to the right of the fridge?", it extracts anchor: fridge, predicate: right-of, metric: 2.0 m. These clauses are passed to the Grounding and Spatial agents for referent resolution and distribution generation.

2. Grounding Agent. The Grounding Agent resolves each anchor referent to a concrete object instance in the 3D scene graph. It uses a combination of string similarity between the referent text and node labels, CLIP-based visual similarity between the referent and object image crops, and a spatial salience prior based on proximity and visibility. A belief distribution over referents is maintained on the shared memory ledger and updated as new observations arrive.

3. Spatial Agent. Once a referent is resolved, the Spatial Agent generates a continuous probability density function over 3D space representing where the goal is likely to be. Directional predicates (e.g., "right of") are modeled as von Mises or radial Gaussian kernels anchored to the resolved object's pose. Metric constraints are modeled as radial Gaussians centered at the predicted distance offset. These analytic kernels are composed by multiplying and renormalizing in log-space, producing a single composed goal distribution.

4. Verifier and Planner. A Verifier checks the composed distribution for coherence and can trigger corrective retries if needed. MAPG masks the density to the navigation mesh and selects its argmax. That point is passed to RRT* as the executable planner goal.

Results

We evaluate MAPG on MAPG-Bench, our metric-semantic goal-grounding benchmark, and use HM-EQA for a separate backend sensitivity check. MAPG-Bench contains 98 frozen object-to-world episodes across 41 HM3D scenes. The primary metric, horizontal SR@1m, measures whether the predicted ground-plane waypoint is within 1 m of reprojected ground truth. Missing predictions count as failures. Horizontal SR@2m is descriptive, while 3D SR retains vertical displacement as a sanity check. All reported MAPG-Bench results use one seed, so they are exploratory.

Across five unoptimized VLM backends, horizontal SR@1m ranges from 17.35% to 21.43%, horizontal SR@2m ranges from 40.82% to 43.88%, and 3D SR@1m ranges from 5.10% to 6.12%. A Luna configuration selected post hoc from legacy confidence-commit failures on this same split reaches 18.37% horizontal SR@1m, 46.94% horizontal SR@2m, and 2.04% 3D SR@1m. It does not improve the primary endpoint over unoptimized Luna at 20.41% and is not an estimate of held-out generalization. The preregistered three-seed analysis, confidence intervals, and error CDF remain incomplete.

In a same-backend comparison rescored under MAPG's reprojected-ground-truth convention, unoptimized Luna reaches 42.86% horizontal SR@2m with MAPG versus 7.14% with adapted GraphEQA. Flash reaches 43.88% with MAPG versus 23.47% with adapted GraphEQA. These are descriptive single-seed comparisons, and the adapted interfaces differ. Our implementation of a LINGO-Space-style baseline disables numeric-literal conditioning under the matched unoptimized Luna control. It reaches 16.33% versus 20.41% at the primary horizontal SR@1m threshold, but 43.88% versus 42.86% at horizontal SR@2m. We treat that threshold tradeoff as a sensitivity result, not causal proof.

On the 94 runnable HM-EQA questions, exact-answer accuracy is 44.68% for Luna, 60.64% for Flash, and 41.49% for GPT 5.2. This is a backend sensitivity check, not a comparison with a published state of the art. A post-freeze MAPG-Bench validator also identified 69 potential annotation or metadata issues, including 12 radial outliers. The frozen bytes remain unchanged for reproducibility, and corrected labels will be versioned. Fourteen above or below queries are scored as ground-plane proxies by the primary horizontal metric, and every backend is 0 of 14 at 3D SR@1m. The Robotis AI Worker demonstration illustrates the approach in one physical environment but is not a systematic real-world evaluation.
